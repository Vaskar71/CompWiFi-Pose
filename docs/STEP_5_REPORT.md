## 1. Bottom line

- **The single-person baseline now provably learns.** All four runs leave the constant regime:
  prediction std across train samples is 0.47–0.49, versus 6.6e-07 for Step-4B-R. They fit the
  training data (train MPJPE 0.12–0.22 × the train mean pose) and generalise to **unseen frames of seen
  groups**: temporal_holdout is 0.23–0.38 × mean pose, and centroid-relative error is 0.70–0.88 × the
  mean skeleton.
- **It does not transfer to unseen groups.** On group_dev (8 candidate-filename groups disjoint from
  train), absolute MPJPE beats the mean pose (0.66–0.85 ×), so the absolute part of G6 passes. That gain
  comes from coarse localisation only: centroid error drops from 0.93 (mean pose) to only 0.59–0.78. The
  **centroid-relative pose is worse than the train-mean skeleton in every run and every seed**
  (1.09–1.25 ×), so G6 fails.
- Selected run **R2** (`simple_cnn`, `legacy_minmax`), three seeds: G1–G5 pass for all seeds and G6 fails
  for all seeds, which gives **YELLOW-POSITION** under the predeclared rule.

## 6. What the controlled comparisons show

- **R2 vs R1 (input normalisation).** Train-split z-score (R1) fits better than legacy per-sample
  min-max (R2): train 0.123 vs 0.157, temporal_holdout 0.204 vs 0.232. On group_dev, R2 is slightly
  better: absolute 0.648 vs 0.659, relative 0.148 vs 0.152. That group_dev gap is about one R2 seed std
  (0.018), so it is weak evidence. Input normalisation did **not** cause the Step-4B-R failure, and
  neither choice fixes unseen-group transfer.
- **R4 vs Step-4B-R (is the legacy decoder usable?).** R4 uses the same unmodified `ViT_Pose_Decoder` and
  the same loss as Step-4B-R. The recipe changed: lr 3e-4 with a 200-step warmup and cosine decay, weight
  decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 1.0, batch 32, 40 epochs. The data changed too: 3,264 samples from 51
  groups instead of 2,048 from 8. Together these remove the collapse: train prediction std is 0.47
  versus 6.6e-07, and G3–G5 pass. So the legacy decoder is trainable, and the Step-4B-R failure came from
  optimisation and/or data scale, not a structurally broken model. Recipe and data changed together, so
  this run cannot separate their effects. R4 remains the weakest fit (train 0.201, holdout 0.338) and has
  the worst group_dev relative error (0.169). The legacy GCN/post-norm decoder is usable but no better
  than an MLP head.
- **R3 (DT-Pose encoder + MLP head)** fits the training data best (0.109) but transfers worst to unseen
  groups (0.800). Trained from scratch on 3,264 samples, it fits group identity more than the CNN does.

## 7. Interpretation (limited to what was measured)

Within seen groups, the models predict both position and body shape from CSI: temporal_holdout
centroid-relative error is 0.70–0.88 × the mean skeleton. On the 8 unseen groups, centroid-relative error
is above the mean skeleton for every model and seed, and centroid error falls only from 0.93 to
0.59–0.78. Each of the 51 training groups is essentially one person at one position (Phase A:
within-group centroid std 0.01–0.12). In that setting, the CSI→pose mapping behaves like recognising a
group or configuration rather than a transferable pose mapping. This step does **not** decide whether
that is a data-scale limit (only 51 distinct training groups) or a fundamental CSI domain shift between
groups.

## 8. Recommendation (per protocol: stop and ask)

The plan's two YELLOW-POSITION options:
- **(a)** Run Phase C under an explicitly disclosed **official-like temporal protocol** (same groups,
  held-out frame blocks, mirroring the public benchmark), always reporting group-disjoint results
  alongside. The baseline already clears that protocol's bar (G5).
- **(b)** A **data-scaling run** on all 23,874 single-person train IDs. This means ≈5–6 GB of
  preprocessing and full training, and needs explicit approval. Caveat: it adds frames per group but no
  new groups. The number of distinct training groups/positions stays at 51, and that is the likely
  limiting factor, so (b) may not change G6.

No follow-up experiment has been started.
